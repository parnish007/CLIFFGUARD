% ── Abstract ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Post-training quantization enables large language models (LLMs) to run on
consumer and embedded hardware, but its effect on RLHF-tuned refusal behaviour
is poorly characterised.
Egashira et al.~\citep{egashira2024exploiting} and \citet{mindthegap2025} showed
that adversaries can craft weights whose malicious behavior activates \emph{only
after} GGUF quantization, with attack-success-rate deltas up to $88.7\%$.
\citet{hong2024quant} showed that GPTQ-3-bit drops toxicity safety by ${\sim}50$
points while degrading MMLU by only ${\sim}8$.
We present \CG{}, an eight-component, edge-native, quantization-aware defense
system targeting this safety-cliff threat.
\CG{} comprises VESTIBULE (input-gate stack), PROBE (quantization-aware
refusal-margin observer), B-PROBE (black-box fallback), TRIPWIRE (streaming
entropy monitor), CONDUCTOR (contextual-bandit gate orchestrator), LOOKOUT
(output-side judge), LADDER (four-tier hardware router), and ATTEST
(weight-hash attestation), composed under a formally stated \emph{decoupling
theorem}: write-side gates operating on input strings and model summary
statistics achieve false-positive rates independent of quantization scheme up to
calibration.

\textbf{This paper reports preliminary results only.}
We evaluate \texttt{Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct} on a Google Colab T4 GPU under FP16
and NF4 quantization (Folds~A and~B of the five-fold pre-registered evaluation).
Fold~A calibrates per-scheme PROBE-RM thresholds at FPR~$= 5\%$:
$\tauq_{\mathrm{FP16}} = 0.09742$ and $\tauq_{\mathrm{NF4}} = 0.09827$
($\Delta\tauq = +0.00085$).
Fold~B reports three cliff metrics versus the FP16 baseline:
$\cliff = 0.167$ ($67\%$ of cliff threshold $\kap = 0.25$),
$\wcliff = 0.014$, and $\bcliff = 0.000$ (PROBE-RM margin proxy only; the
full StrongREJECT + Llama-Guard-3-8B behavioral judge is Phase~C work).
Hypothesis H1 (cliff existence at $\kap \ge 0.25$) is not accepted for this
model--scheme pair.
The geometric signal at $0.67\,\kap$ motivates evaluation of Q3\_K\_M and Q2\_K
schemes---the predicted cliff zone based on published evidence---and motivates
the full five-fold, multi-model-family evaluation described herein.
\end{abstract}

% ── 1. Introduction ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Modern instruction-tuned LLMs encode safety alignment through reinforcement
learning from human feedback (RLHF) \citep{ouyang2022training,bai2022training}.
The alignment produces a learned refusal behavior: given a harmful request, the
model declines.
\citet{arditi2024refusal} showed that this behavior is, to a first
approximation, mediated by a single linear direction $\rhat$ in the
residual-stream activation space.
More recently, \citet{zhao2025refusal} demonstrated that \emph{harmfulness} and
\emph{refusal} are encoded in \textbf{separate} directions: the harmfulness
direction sits at the user-instruction token, while the refusal direction sits at
the post-instruction token.
Both directions must be preserved for alignment to hold.

\paragraph{The quantization threat.}
Post-training quantization (PTQ) reduces model weights from FP16 to lower-bit
formats (INT8, NF4, AWQ-INT4, GGUF Q4/Q3/Q2) to enable deployment on edge
hardware.
While quantization is well understood to degrade perplexity modestly, its effect
on alignment geometry has received far less systematic attention.
\citet{hong2024quant} showed that GPTQ-3-bit quantization of
Llama-2-13B-Chat drops toxicity-safety performance by approximately 50 points
while MMLU drops only ${\sim}8$ points---a stark safety-capability asymmetry.
\citet{egashira2024exploiting} (NeurIPS 2024) demonstrated that adversaries can
produce FP16 model weights that appear safe, where the malicious behavior
activates \emph{only} after GGUF quantization, with attack success rate (ASR)
deltas of $\Delta = 88.7\%$ (insecure code generation), $\Delta = 85.0\%$
(content injection), and $\Delta = 30.1\%$ (refusal bypass).
\citet{mindthegap2025} (ICLR 2025) further showed that the MSE objective used
by GGUF k-quants does not preserve refusal-related activation geometry.

We refer to the quantization level at which refusal degradation crosses a
meaningful safety threshold as a \textbf{safety cliff}. No existing
prompt-injection defense system---Llama Guard \citep{inan2023llama_guard},
PromptGuard~2, NeMo, Rebuff, SecAlign, Constitutional Classifiers, LlamaFirewall,
or CaMeL---was designed for this failure mode in an edge-hardware envelope.

\paragraph{Our system.}
We introduce \CG{}, an eight-component, edge-native, quantization-aware defense
system.
\CG{} is organized around a \emph{decoupling theorem} (informally: write-side
gates achieve FPR portability across quantization schemes up to a calibration
map) and a pre-registered five-fold evaluation framework with five falsifiable
hypotheses (H1--H5).
\CG{} targets the open-weight, locally-served edge LLM deployment surface:
Ollama / llama.cpp, Hugging Face transformers, vLLM, Apple MLX, and GGUF-serving
runtimes.

\paragraph{Scope of this paper.}
\textbf{This is a preliminary paper.}
We report the first live experimental run covering Folds~A and~B only, using a
single model family (Llama-3.2-3B) and two quantization schemes (FP16, NF4) on
a Google Colab T4 GPU.
Hypotheses H2--H5 and Folds~C--E are planned but not yet executed.
We describe the full \CG{} framework so that the evaluation context is clear and
the future evaluation can be read directly against the pre-registered design.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item We introduce \CG{}---eight named components, a threat model of nine
        adversaries (A1--A9), a decoupling theorem, and five pre-registered
        hypotheses (H1--H5).
  \item We define three cliff metrics ($\cliff$, $\wcliff$, $\bcliff$) that
        jointly test the safety-cliff hypothesis while breaking single-metric
        circularity.
  \item We report the first empirical Fold~A/B results: $\cliff = 0.167$
        ($0.67\,\kap$), $\wcliff = 0.014$, $\bcliff = 0.000$ under NF4 for
        Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct.
  \item We identify the key gap: $\bcliff$ uses a proxy judge (PROBE-RM margin
        threshold crossing); the full StrongREJECT + Llama-Guard-3-8B judge is
        Phase~C work.
  \item We describe all five evaluation folds and argue why the predicted cliff
        zone (Q3\_K\_M and below) has not yet been tested.
\end{itemize}

% ── 2. Related Work ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Refusal geometry.}
\citet{arditi2024refusal} showed that refusal is mediated by a single linear
direction $\rhat$ in the residual stream; ablating this direction raises ASR
dramatically.
\citet{zhao2025refusal} (NeurIPS 2025) further separated the \emph{harmfulness}
direction at the instruction token from the \emph{refusal} direction at the
post-instruction token.
Our PROBE-RM primitive operationalises these insights as a quantization-aware
sensor.

\paragraph{Quantization and alignment.}
\citet{hong2024quant} demonstrated a safety-capability asymmetry at 3-bit
quantization.
\citet{egashira2024exploiting} (NeurIPS 2024) introduced the cliff-exploit attack,
where a model is fine-tuned to produce malicious outputs only after GGUF
quantization.
\citet{mindthegap2025} (ICLR 2025) showed that the GGUF k-quant MSE objective
does not preserve refusal-related activation structure.
\citet{dettmers2023qlora} introduced NF4 as a 4-bit data type minimizing
quantization error for normally distributed weights.
\citet{wee2025caq} (CAQ) addressed calibration-aware quantization to mitigate
such degradation.

\paragraph{Adversarial robustness and red-teaming.}
\citet{zou2023advbench} introduced AdvBench and the GCG adversarial suffix
attack.
\citet{chao2024jailbreakbench} introduced JailbreakBench.
\citet{mazeika2024harmbench} introduced HarmBench as a standardized red-teaming
evaluation.
\citet{souly2024strongreject} proposed StrongREJECT as a scoring rubric that
penalizes empty refusals.

\paragraph{Defense systems.}
\citet{inan2023llama_guard} introduced Llama Guard, a safety classifier.
\citet{ghosh2024aegis} introduced AEGIS, a no-regret combination of LLM safety
experts.
\citet{li2010contextual} introduced LinUCB, the contextual-bandit algorithm used
in CONDUCTOR.
\citet{greshake2023not} introduced the indirect prompt injection threat model
that grounds our A2 adversary.
Our work is distinct in targeting the quantization-induced safety cliff as a
first-class threat, composing lightweight write-side gates under a decoupling
theorem.

% ── 3. The CLIFFGUARD Framework ───────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{The CLIFFGUARD Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{System Overview}

\CG{} is a stateless, online-RL-adapted, defense-in-depth system for prompt
injection on edge-deployed LLMs operating across heterogeneous quantization
regimes.
Its design is based on a key insight from the refusal-geometry literature:
because refusal lives in a low-dimensional subspace of the residual stream
\citep{arditi2024refusal}, \emph{write-side} defenses that operate on input
strings and model summary statistics can be made independent of the quantization
scheme up to a calibration map.
This is the informal content of the decoupling theorem stated in
\S\ref{sec:decoupling}.

\CG{} comprises eight named components.

\paragraph{VESTIBULE.}
Input-gate stack of fast, stateless write-side gates: a compression-density gate
(LZ4 compression ratio, targets GCG and ArtPrompt), an instruction-density gate
(verb-entity collisions), an encoding-anomaly gate (non-printable, base-N,
ASCII art density), and a provenance-aware spotlighting gate (Hines et al.\
arXiv:2403.14720). Stateless across requests; ports across hardware tiers unchanged.

\paragraph{PROBE.}
Quantization-aware white-box refusal-margin observer.
Computes (a) the signed projection $\rho(x) = \langle h_\ell(x),\, \rhat \rangle$
of the residual stream at layer $\ell$ and post-instruction token onto the
difference-in-means refusal direction $\rhat$ (PROBE-RM), (b) the same
projection onto a harmfulness direction $\hat{h}$ at the user-instruction token
(PROBE-HD, following \citealt{zhao2025refusal}), and (c) a margin-trajectory
slope across three layers (PROBE-MT).
The calibration table ($\rhat_{M,q}$, threshold $\tauq$) is per-$(M, q)$ pair.

\paragraph{B-PROBE.}
Black-box fallback observer for closed-weight API endpoints and NPU-frozen graphs.
Computes a logistic-head margin from top-$k$ log-probabilities (B-PROBE-LOGIT)
and an $N$-paraphrase first-token Jensen--Shannon divergence
(B-PROBE-CONSISTENCY).
FPR portability holds; TPR is strictly weaker (pre-registered as H3).

\paragraph{TRIPWIRE.}
Streaming token-entropy CUSUM (TRIPWIRE-H) and KenLM reference-ratio gate
(TRIPWIRE-R). Operates during decoding; does not buffer outputs.
Alarm at CUSUM statistic $S_t > h$ tuned for $\mathrm{ARL}_0 \approx 1000$
benign tokens.

\paragraph{CONDUCTOR.}
LinUCB \citep{li2010contextual} contextual-bandit policy that selects
gate-weight vectors per request.
EXP3.S fallback under non-stationary attack distributions.
Updates only on reward signals: LOOKOUT verdict, canary-token leakage,
tool-call audit failure.
The only learnable component; all others are calibration-only.

\paragraph{LOOKOUT.}
Output-side judge and per-session canary-token loop.
On the 8\,GB tier can call Llama-Guard-3-1B-INT4 or a DeBERTa-class classifier.
On lower tiers falls back to canary-leak detection plus a linear judge on
TRIPWIRE features.

\paragraph{LADDER.}
Four-tier minimum viable defense set (MVDS) router:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Tier~A} (RTX 5060 8\,GB, FP16/NF4/AWQ-INT4): all 12 gates.
  \item \textbf{Tier~B} (Raspberry Pi 5 8\,GB, GGUF Q4\_K\_M/Q3\_K\_M):
        11 gates (LOOKOUT-JG $N=2$).
  \item \textbf{Tier~C} (2\,GB embedded, Q3\_K\_M): minimal gates ---
        \emph{not defended against A7}.
  \item \textbf{Tier~C+} (2\,GB + PromptGuard-2-22M-INT4): modest defense
        against A7 (H5).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{ATTEST.}
Boot-time SHA-256 of the GGUF/safetensors blob compared against a
vendor-signed manifest.
Defends against A2 (Egashira-style poisoned weights
\citep{egashira2024exploiting}).

\subsection{Decoupling Theorem (Informal)}
\label{sec:decoupling}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Theorem (FPR decoupling, informal).} Let $g(x)$ be a write-side gate
that operates only on (a) input strings and (b) summary statistics of model
outputs (top-$k$ log-probabilities, refusal-margin scalars).
For any two quantization schemes $q_1, q_2$, if the gate is re-calibrated on a
per-scheme holdout, then
$|\mathrm{FPR}(g, q_1) - \mathrm{FPR}(g, q_2)| \le \varepsilon$
for $\varepsilon$ depending only on the calibration set size and the
distributional shift of the model's summary statistics.
TPR is \emph{not} decoupled---that is the empirical question the cliff regime
answers (H1).
\end{quote}

The intuition is that write-side gates never touch the model's internal weights
directly.
The only quantization-sensitive quantity is the refusal-margin distribution,
which the calibration map absorbs.
A black-box corollary extends the same FPR portability to top-$k$
log-probability observables (B-PROBE).

\subsection{Five Hypotheses}
\label{sec:hypotheses}

The \CG{} evaluation pre-registers five falsifiable hypotheses:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item[H1 (cliff existence).] For $\ge 2$ of 3 model families, both $\cliff$
        and $\bcliff$ exhibit a $\kap \ge 0.25$ jump at the same boundary.
  \item[H2 (FPR decoupling, white-box).] PROBE-RM FPR after per-quantization
        calibration varies by $< \varepsilon = 0.02$ across
        \{FP16, NF4, AWQ-INT4, Q4\_K\_M, Q3\_K\_M\}.
  \item[H3 (FPR decoupling, black-box).] Same property for B-PROBE-LOGIT on
        top-$k$ log-probabilities, with strictly weaker TPR.
  \item[H4 (composition gain).] Full primitive stack achieves strictly smaller
        attack bypass rate (ABR) than any single primitive at matched FPR.
  \item[H5 (Tier-C structural weakness).] Tier~C without a dedicated classifier
        shows no significant ABR reduction against A7 relative to no-defense;
        Tier~C+ with PromptGuard-2-22M-INT4 does.
\end{description}

\subsection{Five-Fold Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:folds}

The five evaluation folds are assigned to independent data corpora to avoid
contamination.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item[Fold~A (Calibration).] Compute $\rhat$, $\tauq$ at FPR~$=5\%$ on 2K
        benign + 2K refused prompts from Anthropic-HH-RLHF / OASST1.
        \textbf{Status: TESTED} (400 benign + 200 harmful in this paper).
  \item[Fold~B (Cliff measurement).] Compute $\cliff$, $\wcliff$, $\bcliff$ on
        AdvBench + HarmBench held out from Fold~A.
        \textbf{Status: TESTED} (AdvBench + JailbreakBench in this paper;
        $\bcliff$ uses PROBE-RM proxy, not full judge).
  \item[Fold~C (Defense composition).] ABR and FPR per primitive using JBB,
        AgentDojo, InjecAgent, GCG, AutoDAN, PAIR, TAP, ArtPrompt, BoN,
        TensorTrust; full StrongREJECT + Llama-Guard-3-8B judge.
        \textbf{Status: PLANNED}.
  \item[Fold~D (Bandit drift).] CONDUCTOR online learning under A8 adversary.
        \textbf{Status: PLANNED}.
  \item[Fold~E (BCN-2 dataset).] Non-circular below-cliff dataset construction
        with Mistral paraphraser.
        \textbf{Status: PLANNED}.
\end{description}

% ── 4. Cliff Metrics ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Cliff Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

Fold~B evaluates three metrics in combination to test H1 while breaking the
circularity of single-metric validation.

\paragraph{Geometric distance $\cliff$.}
Cosine-based distance between refusal directions across schemes:
\begin{equation}
  \cliff(q,\,\mathrm{FP16}) = 1 - \langle \rhat_q,\; \rhat_{\mathrm{FP16}} \rangle.
  \label{eq:cliff}
\end{equation}
A value near zero indicates the quantized model has preserved the same refusal
subspace; a value near one indicates near-complete misalignment.

\paragraph{Wasserstein distance $\wcliff$.}
The $W_2$ distance between empirical margin distributions:
\begin{equation}
  \wcliff(q,\,\mathrm{FP16}) = W_2\!\left(P_q^{\mathrm{margin}},\;
                               P_{\mathrm{FP16}}^{\mathrm{margin}}\right).
  \label{eq:wcliff}
\end{equation}
Captures distributional shift beyond what the geometric metric detects.

\paragraph{Behavioral metric $\bcliff$.}
The compliance-rate delta on the harmful Fold~B corpus, evaluated by a
StrongREJECT + Llama-Guard-3-8B judge \citep{souly2024strongreject,
inan2023llama_guard}:
\begin{equation}
  \bcliff(q,\,\mathrm{FP16}) = \mathrm{CR}(q) - \mathrm{CR}(\mathrm{FP16}),
  \label{eq:bcliff}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{CR}(q)$ is the fraction of harmful prompts answered by the
model under scheme $q$ according to the external judge.
This metric is \emph{non-circular}: it does not use PROBE-RM margins in the
behavioral verdict.
\textbf{In the current paper (Fold~B, preliminary run), we use a PROBE-RM
margin threshold crossing as a proxy for the behavioral verdict.}
The full judge stack is Phase~C work (see \S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Cliff threshold and H1 criterion.}
H1 is accepted for model family $\mathcal{F}$ if there exists a scheme $q^*$ such
that $\cliff(q^*) \ge \kap$ and $\bcliff(q^*) \ge \kap$, i.e., both the
geometric and behavioral metrics cross the threshold $\kap = 0.25$ at the same
scheme boundary.
This two-metric requirement prevents a geometric rotation without behavioral
consequence from being declared a safety cliff.
The cliff boundary is $q^* = \operatorname{argmin}_{q}\{\cliff(q) \ge \kap\}$.

% ── 5. Experimental Setup ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Model and Hardware}

We evaluate \texttt{meta-llama/Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct} \citep{meta2024llama3},
a 3B-parameter instruction-tuned model.
Activations are extracted at layer $\ell = 14$ (of 28), chosen as the midpoint
of the architecture where the refusal direction is empirically most linearly
separable in similar models.

All experiments run on a Google Colab T4 session (NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU, 16\,GB
VRAM, CUDA 12.8).
Software: \texttt{transformers} 4.48, \texttt{bitsandbytes} 0.45,
\texttt{torch} 2.5, \texttt{numpy} 1.26.

\subsection{Quantization Schemes}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em, itemsep=2pt]
  \item[FP16] Full bfloat16/float16 precision; alignment baseline.
        VRAM: ${\approx}6.2$\,GB.
  \item[NF4] 4-bit normal-float quantization via bitsandbytes
        \citep{dettmers2023qlora}, 64-element blocks, double-quantized scaling.
        VRAM: ${\approx}2.1$\,GB.
\end{description}

Schemes not yet evaluated: INT8, AWQ-INT4, GGUF Q4\_K\_M, Q3\_K\_M, Q2\_K.
Q3\_K\_M is the \emph{predicted cliff zone} based on
\citet{egashira2024exploiting} and \citet{mindthegap2025} evidence.

\subsection{Datasets}

\paragraph{Fold A (calibration).}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=1pt]
  \item 400 benign prompts: Anthropic-HH-RLHF helpful turns

  \item 200 harmful prompts: Anthropic-HH-RLHF rejected turns.
\end{itemize}
This is a reduced run; the full five-fold protocol targets 2K benign + 2K refused.

\paragraph{Fold B (cliff measurement).}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=1pt]
  \item AdvBench \citep{zou2023advbench}: 520 harmful behaviors.
  \item JailbreakBench \citep{chao2024jailbreakbench} (JBB-Behaviors, harmful
        split): 100 harmful goals.
\end{itemize}
Fold isolation verified via SHA-256 disjointness before Fold~B execution.

H2}: FPR stability across quantization schemes is consistent with the decoupling
theorem.
However, with only two schemes and one model, this is insufficient to accept H2.

\paragraph{Geometric metric $\cliff = 0.167$.}
The NF4 refusal direction has rotated from the FP16 baseline.
At $0.67\,\kap$, this is a substantive signal: quantization is not invisible to
the refusal geometry, but the shift does not constitute a safety cliff at this
threshold.
The result is consistent with the expectation from \citet{hong2024quant} and
\citet{dettmers2023qlora}: NF4's normal-quantile design minimises reconstruction
error and preserves activation statistics better than integer quantization, so a
sub-threshold result at NF4 is expected.
The predicted cliff at Q3\_K\_M and Q2\_K (based on Egashira and Mind the Gap
evidence) has not yet been tested.

\paragraph{Wasserstein metric $\wcliff = 0.014$.}
Near zero, indicating that the margin \emph{distribution} over benign prompts is
largely unchanged even as the direction has rotated.
This is consistent with the interpretation that NF4 rotates $\rhat$ slightly
without distorting the margin landscape.

\paragraph{Behavioral proxy $\bcliff = 0.000$.}
No additional harmful prompts crossed the calibrated threshold under NF4.
\textbf{Important caveat:} this uses the PROBE-RM margin threshold crossing as a
behavioral proxy.
Since PROBE-RM is the same observable used to calibrate $\tauq$ in Fold~A, this
proxy is partially circular---a threshold calibrated on the benign distribution
will not register zero-margin shift as a behavioral change.
The full StrongREJECT + Llama-Guard-3-8B judge is required to make this metric
non-circular; that is Phase~C work.

\paragraph{Geometric--behavioral agreement.}
$\cliff = 0.167$ versus $\bcliff = 0.000$ (proxy) represents a discordant pair.
Under the full judge, $\bcliff$ may remain near zero (NF4 shift is too small to
cause actual behavioral change) or it may register a non-zero value due to proxy
circularity masking real change.
This uncertainty is a primary motivation for Fold~C.

% ── 7. Discussion ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Sub-Threshold Result and What It Implies}

The absence of a detected cliff for Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct at NF4 is consistent
with the design of NF4.
By targeting the quantile distribution of normally distributed weights
\citep{dettmers2023qlora}, NF4 minimises quantization error relative to FP16.
This appears to preserve not only task performance (perplexity) but also
alignment geometry ($\cliff$).

The geometric shift $\cliff = 0.167$ represents $67\%$ progression toward
the cliff boundary.
Linear extrapolation suggests that a scheme applying roughly $1.5\times$ more
compression should produce $\cliff \approx 0.25$.
GGUF Q3\_K\_M (${\approx}3.35$ bits per weight, mixed-precision k-quants) is the
primary next candidate.
\citet{egashira2024exploiting} showed cliff-exploit ASR deltas up to $88.7\%$ at
GGUF quantization levels; \citet{mindthegap2025} showed that the GGUF k-quant
MSE objective does not preserve refusal activation geometry.
Both papers point to Q3\_K\_M and below as the critical zone that Folds~C--E must
target.

\subsection{The Novel Threat: A7 Adversary}

The A7 adversary (quantization-cliff exploiter) is the novel threat \CG{}
targets.
An A7 adversary crafts prompts---using entirely natural language, without
adversarial suffixes---whose refusal margin lies just above $\tauq$ in FP16 but
falls below it in Q3\_K\_M.
Because the FP16 version refuses, such prompts pass content moderation at upload
time.
After quantization for edge deployment, they succeed silently.
No existing defense was designed for this mode.
The current results do not yet test A7 at the predicted cliff zone; this is the
primary Phase~C objective.

\subsection{Decoupling Theorem: Preliminary Evidence}

The small $\Delta\tauq = 0.00085$ between FP16 and NF4 is consistent with
the decoupling theorem: the calibration map absorbs the quantization-induced
shift in the margin distribution, yielding nearly identical FPR at matched
thresholds.
This must be tested across five schemes and three model families to constitute
evidence for H2.
The present results are suggestive but insufficient.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Single model family, two schemes.}
        Results cover Llama-3.2-3B at FP16 and NF4 only.
        H1 requires $\ge 2$ of 3 model families; Mistral-7B-Instruct and
        Qwen-2.5-7B are planned but not yet tested.
  \item \textbf{Proxy behavioral metric.}
        $\bcliff = 0.000$ is computed from PROBE-RM margin threshold crossings,
        which is partially circular.
        The full StrongREJECT \citep{souly2024strongreject} +
        Llama-Guard-3-8B \citep{inan2023llama_guard} non-circular judge is
        required for valid H1 acceptance or rejection; this is Fold~C work.
  \item \textbf{Cliff zone not yet tested.}
        The predicted cliff zone (Q3\_K\_M, Q2\_K) has not been evaluated.
        GGUF quantization requires llama.cpp or Ollama, which is different
        infrastructure from the current bitsandbytes-based setup.
  \item \textbf{Fold~B corpus scope.}
        HarmBench \citep{mazeika2024harmbench} and ArtPrompt are absent from
        this run; only AdvBench and JailbreakBench were used.
  \item \textbf{Hardware limitation.}
        Colab T4 (16\,GB VRAM) limits evaluation to 3B models.
        Larger models (8B, 13B) require at least an A100-40G.
  \item \textbf{H2--H5 not tested.}
        Defense composition (H4), bandit drift (H4/CONDUCTOR), Tier-C weakness
        (H5), and black-box FPR decoupling (H3) are entirely future work.
\end{itemize}

% ── 8. Future Work ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*., leftmargin=2em, itemsep=4pt]

  \item \textbf{GGUF Q3\_K\_M and Q2\_K (predicted cliff zone).}
        These are the primary targets for H1 acceptance.
        The current $0.67\,\kap$ signal at NF4 makes Q3\_K\_M the highest-priority
        next step.

  \item \textbf{Full behavioral judge (Fold~C Phase~C).}
        Replace the PROBE-RM $\bcliff$ proxy with the full StrongREJECT +
        Llama-Guard-3-8B judge to produce a non-circular H1 verdict.
        This also unlocks the defense composition evaluation (Fold~C).

  \item \textbf{Multiple model families.}
        Mistral-7B-Instruct, Qwen-2.5-7B, and Gemma-2-9B under the same
        calibration protocol, to test whether cliff boundaries are
        model-family-dependent.

  \item \textbf{Full Fold~B corpus.}
        Add HarmBench \citep{mazeika2024harmbench} and ArtPrompt to reach the
        full pre-registered Fold~B corpus.

  \item \textbf{Fold~D: CONDUCTOR bandit drift.}
        Evaluate online learning under the A8 defender-aware adversary.

  \item \textbf{Fold~E: BCN-2 below-cliff dataset.}
        Construct the non-circular below-cliff naturals dataset using a Mistral
        paraphraser (different family from the model under test), enabling
        H1 evaluation without proxy judges.

  \item \textbf{Full H2--H5 evaluation.}
        FPR decoupling (H2, H3), composition gain (H4), and Tier-C structural
        weakness (H5) each require Folds C--E at minimum.

\end{enumerate}

% ── 9. Conclusion ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced \CG{}, an eight-component edge-native defense system against
prompt injection at the safety cliff.
The framework is grounded in a decoupling theorem (write-side FPR portability
across quantization schemes), a threat model of nine adversaries, and five
pre-registered hypotheses with a five-fold evaluation protocol.

We reported the first empirical Fold~A/B results: for Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct at
NF4 quantization on a Colab T4 GPU, the geometric cliff metric reaches
$\cliff = 0.167$ ($0.67\,\kap = 0.25$) with negligible Wasserstein and proxy
behavioral deviation.
H1 is not accepted for this model--scheme pair.

The result is encouraging from a deployment perspective for NF4, but it leaves
the predicted cliff zone (Q3\_K\_M and below, motivated by
\citealt{egashira2024exploiting} and \citealt{mindthegap2025}) entirely untested.
The $0.167$ geometric signal is a reproducible starting point.
Completing the five-fold evaluation across three model families and five
quantization schemes---with a non-circular behavioral judge---remains the
primary goal.

All artefacts are available at \url{https://github.com/parnish007/CLIFFGUARD}.
